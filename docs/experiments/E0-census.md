# E0: census of the ClamAV main, daily and bytecode databases

E0 (docs/DESIGN.md section 14) replaces the design's estimates with counts taken from the real
databases. Its numbers drive the hash index (size-less signatures, size coverage), atom selection
(tiers A to D), signature names (SIGMETA and NAME_POOL) and functionality-level filtering.

## Method

- `zig build census -Doptimize=fast -- main.cvd daily.cvd bytecode.cvd` (tools/census.zig over
  src/compile/census.zig). Every CVD is verified first (MD5 and Talos's RSA signature), then read
  member by member and line by line through the compiler's own parsers (`hashsig`, `ndb`, `ldb`,
  `ftm`, `cdb`). Nothing per signature is kept except 64-bit hashes for the distinct counts: the
  whole census runs in 3.8 s and 248 MB RSS on an Apple M3 Pro.
- The databases are fetched on each host (here with Cisco's cvdupdate) and never committed. The
  report below holds statistics only.
- Rerunning the census on the same databases gives a byte-identical report: every table is sorted
  and every hash is seeded with a constant. Checked by running it twice and comparing (`cmp`).
- Definitions:
  - *Longest fixed run*: consecutive literal bytes inside one part of a hex subsignature, after the
    compile-time expansion of modifiers and small alternations (`hexsig.expand`); the weakest
    variant decides. Masked nibbles, `{n}` skips, alternations and part breaks end a run. Nocase
    literals count (they are fixed after folding); a `::w` literal of n characters is 2n bytes.
  - *Tiers*: A 11 bytes or more, B 7 to 10, C 4 to 6, D 3 or fewer (docs/DESIGN.md 4.2).
  - *Tier D deferrable candidates*: tier-D subsigs in a logical signature that also has a tier
    A-C hex subsig. Whether that partner is *required* by the expression is E3's question (#34).

## Findings

1. **Size-less hash signatures exist, and they defeat hash skipping on their own.** There are 13
   (11 MD5, 2 SHA-256, all in main.hsb, all `*` with level 73). One size-less MD5 is enough to
   put MD5 in the whole-file scope's `sizeless_mask`, so every file is MD5-hashed and
   SHA-256-hashed whatever its size (PE section hashing stays size-gated). ClamAV pays the same cost for the same reason. The size map still gates
   SHA-1 completely and decides which *tables* are probed; E1 (#10) measures the cost.
2. **3,212,898 hash signatures**, 83% of them PE section MD5s (`.mdb`, 2,670,863). Whole-file
   MD5s: 541,800. SHA-1 and SHA-256 together: 238. No `.imp` import hashes ship.
3. **Sizes below 2^24 cover 98.7% of whole-file MD5 entries and 99.99% of section entries.**
   Whole-file MD5s use 174,283 distinct sizes (1% of the 2^24 dense range), sections 69,636. The
   largest single-size bucket holds 4,190 file entries and 38,871 section entries.
4. **Body subsignatures are mostly long**: 98% of `.ndb` subsigs are tier A. Logical subsigs are
   not: 57% A, 17% B, **26% C** and 0.17% D. Tier C is the candidate-volume risk, not tier D: a
   six-byte run is the single commonest length (386,632 subsigs). Only 2,520 logical and 476 body subsigs are tier D, and 2,048 plus
   198 of those carry a fixed offset.
5. **Names tokenise almost completely, but not with the shape the engine assumed.** 79% of names
   are `Family-N` (one number) and 21% `Family-N-R`. The tokeniser in `ir.NameTokens` only
   accepts `Family-N-R`, so 2.9 million names were stored verbatim (65 MB of NAME_POOL). With
   both shapes, every one of the 3,641,273 names tokenises into 308,730 distinct families
   (5.8 MB). The largest number is 10,060,800 (under 2^24) and the largest revision 12.
6. **PCRE is rare**: 1,157 PCRE subsigs in 1,135 logical signatures; one byte-compare subsig.
7. **Functionality levels**: no hash, `.ldb` or `.cdb` rule is outside the engine's level 220.
   161 `.ndb` rules and 12 `.ftm` rules are retired before it (maximum below 220) and are skipped.
8. **320 lines do not parse** out of 3,651,997: 111 `fuzzy_img` and 81 icon subsigs, 55
   regexes zig-regex rejects, 19 `.cdb` regexes that need backtracking, 14 `.ftm` lines with
   offsets or types the engine does not know, and a handful of malformed bodies. Every one is in
   the coverage report with its reason. Every hash line parses.
9. **Bytecode**: 85 programs (bytecode.cvd's header says 80), 0.0023% of signatures.
